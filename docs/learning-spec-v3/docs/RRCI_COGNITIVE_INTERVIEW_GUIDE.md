# RRCI cognitive interview guide

## Purpose

Collect response-process validity evidence: do participants interpret each item and distractor in the way the designers intend?

## Sampling

Explorer interviews should intentionally include:
- age 14–15;
- age 16–17;
- adults with no specialist nuclear training.

Scientist interviews should include STEM university students and/or professionals with enough background to expose scientific ambiguity.

## Procedure

1. Explain that **the questionnaire is being tested, not the participant**.
2. Ask participant to think aloud while answering.
3. Do not correct them until the item's reasoning has been captured.
4. Probe neutrally.

Standard probes:
- "Che cosa significa questa domanda con parole tue?"
- "Quali informazioni hai usato?"
- "Cosa ti ha fatto scartare l'opzione X?"
- "C'è qualche parola che cambieresti?"
- "Hai esitato tra due opzioni? Perché?"
- "Questa risposta la sapevi, l'hai dedotta, o hai tirato a indovinare?"

For two-tier items:
- capture tier 1 choice before showing tier 2;
- examine whether tier 2 reveals a misconception hidden by a correct tier 1 guess.

## Coding per item

- intended reasoning observed: yes/no/partial
- terminology understood: yes/no
- unintended shortcut: yes/no
- multiple defensible answers perceived: yes/no
- distractor linked to target misconception: yes/no
- level too easy / appropriate / too hard
- revise wording: yes/no

## Stop rule

Cognitive interviewing is iterative. Revise items between small waves rather than interviewing 20 people on wording already known to be defective.
