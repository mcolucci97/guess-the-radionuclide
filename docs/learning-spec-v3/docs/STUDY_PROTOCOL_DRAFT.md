# Draft scientific study protocol — INFN

**Status: design draft only. Requires INFN scientific/privacy approval before real research collection.**

## Working title

Learning and science communication about radioactivity through a radionuclide deduction game.

## Central research question

Can a deduction game based on radionuclides produce measurable learning and science-communication outcomes about radioactivity and its applications during a single structured gameplay session?

The primary claim is NOT "repeated gaming improves memory".

## Communication objective

Test whether the game broadens the participant's scientific representation of radioactivity beyond an exclusive association with danger, accidents, weapons and nuclear energy, while preserving accurate understanding of radiological risk.

Desired additional associations include medicine, environment, natural radioactivity, cosmogenic/astronomical contexts, dating/geoscience, industry and scientific research.

## Population

Primary planned population: age 14+; school groups are strategically important; adults/university populations may also be studied.

## Proposed first pilot

- short baseline profile;
- pre-test;
- one gameplay session;
- post-test;
- usability/engagement items;
- optional delayed retention test if feasible.

## Proposed controlled study

### Control
Core gameplay without Learning Engine interventions.

### Intervention
Same game plus Learning Engine V1.

This isolates the effect of learning design better than comparing game vs lecture.

Randomization may be individual or cluster/class-based; school contamination and operational constraints should determine the final design.

## Primary outcomes

1. conceptual understanding of radioactivity;
2. decay/radiation distinction;
3. half-life reasoning;
4. medical application knowledge (PET/SPECT/therapy);
5. breadth of radioactivity applications;
6. reduction of selected misconceptions.

## Secondary outcomes

- quality/discriminative value of questions;
- transfer to unfamiliar radionuclides/cases;
- engagement;
- time on task;
- optional delayed retention.

## Suggested pre/post structure

8–12 short objective items plus one controlled open/association item.

Do not treat self-selected Explorer/Scientist level as a substitute for pre-test knowledge.

## Association breadth item

Example: "Indicate or describe contexts in which radioactivity can be relevant, encountered or used."

Coding categories:
- danger/accidents;
- weapons;
- energy;
- medicine;
- environment;
- natural radioactivity;
- astronomy/cosmogenic;
- dating/geoscience;
- industry;
- scientific research.

Success is not reduced awareness of danger; it is increased scientifically correct breadth.

## Baseline covariates

Use broad categories: age band, education band, STEM/non-STEM, previous formal exposure to radioactivity, selected game level.

## Analysis principle

Pre-register primary outcomes, scoring rules, exclusion criteria, missing-data handling and subgroup analyses before analyzing the definitive study.
