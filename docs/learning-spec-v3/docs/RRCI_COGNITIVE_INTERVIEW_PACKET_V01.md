# RRCI Cognitive Interview Packet V0.1

Run only after first expert revisions.

## Goal

Determine whether students interpret each item as intended, not whether they merely select the keyed answer.

## Suggested sample

Explorer: 10–15 students across the lower and upper end of the intended age/background range.
Scientist: 6–10 advanced STEM/university participants.

## Procedure

Use think-aloud sparingly, then standardized probes:
- “Con parole tue, cosa ti sta chiedendo questa domanda?”
- “Come hai scelto questa risposta?”
- “Cosa significa per te [termine chiave]?”
- “C'era un'altra risposta che ti sembrava possibile?”
- “Quale opzione ti ha fatto escludere le altre?”
- “Questa domanda usa parole che non useresti normalmente?”

Do not teach the answer before the participant has completed the reasoning.

## High-priority V0.1 probes

### E10 — irradiation / PET / food
Ask separately what the participant thinks happens to:
- irradiated food;
- a person standing near a PET patient;
- the PET patient themselves.

Check specifically whether “the PET patient is radioactive” is incorrectly generalized to “anyone irradiated by the patient becomes radioactive”.

### E18–E21 — decay mechanisms
Check whether:
- alpha is recognized as a helium nucleus rather than a generic high-energy particle;
- beta- electron is wrongly imagined as a pre-existing orbital electron;
- beta+ is understood as positron emission rather than proton emission;
- EC is understood as a nuclear transformation rather than ordinary electronic capture with no nuclear change.

### E40 — breadth
Do not prompt categories. After the answer, ask what the participant meant by each example. This is needed to distinguish, for example, environmental contamination from natural environmental radioactivity.

## Revision rule

If several participants reach the keyed answer using reasoning outside the intended construct, revise or remove the item even if percent-correct looks good.
