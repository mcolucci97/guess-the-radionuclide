# Bibliography — measurable outcomes and test implementation

This is a design bibliography, not yet a PRISMA systematic review.

## Radioactivity misconceptions / conceptual understanding

- Morales López, A. I., & Tuzón Marco, P. (2022). *Misconceptions, Knowledge, and Attitudes Towards the Phenomenon of Radioactivity*. Science & Education, 31, 405–426. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11191-021-00251-w  
  Key use: natural/artificial misconception; radioactivity/radiation/material confusion; irradiation vs contamination; accumulation; danger-only representation; applications breadth.

- Siersma, P. T., Pol, H. J., van Joolingen, W. R., & Visscher, A. J. (2021). *Pre-university students’ conceptions regarding radiation and radioactivity in a medical context*. International Journal of Science Education, 43(2), 179–196. https://doi.org/10.1080/09500693.2020.1864504  
  Key use: persistent irradiation/contamination confusion and medical-context misconceptions.

- Prather, E. (2005). *Students' beliefs about the role of atoms in radioactive decay and half-life*. Journal of Geoscience Education, 53(4), 345–354. https://doi.org/10.5408/1089-9995-53.4.345  
  Key use: half-life and decay reasoning difficulties.

- Nakiboglu, C., & Tekin, B. B. (2006). *Identifying Students' Misconceptions about Nuclear Chemistry. A Study of Turkish High School Students*. Journal of Chemical Education, 83, 1712. https://doi.org/10.1021/ed083p1712  
  Key use: high-school misconceptions in half-life, nuclear stability, decay, applications and atom/element reasoning.

## Nuclear/radiochemistry game-based education

- Lu, S. E., et al. (2025). *Decked Out for Success: A Novel Card Game to Support School Teaching of Radioactivity and Nuclear Science*. Journal of Chemical Education. https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jchemed.4c00603  
  Key use: closest card-game precedent; good feasibility/engagement evidence but learning measures are substantially self-reported.

- Lu, S. E., et al. (2025). *Global lessons: a comparative analysis of nuclear gamification's impact in American and British students*. Journal of Radioanalytical and Nuclear Chemistry. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10967-025-10455-x  
  Key use: cross-context RAD Ratings follow-up and methodological limitations.

- *Radioactive World: An Outreach Activity for Nuclear Chemistry* (2019). Journal of Chemical Education. https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jchemed.9b00242  
  Key use: Isotope Rummy / outreach precedent.

- *Radioactivity and U-235: An Educational Card Game to Introduce Nuclear Decay and the Benefits, Risks, and Safe Handling Strategies for Radionuclides* (2023). Journal of Chemical Education. https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jchemed.3c00478  
  Key use: card-game precedent and perception/familiarity outcomes.

## Concept inventory development / validation

- Netere, A. K., Babey, A.-M., Kelly-Laubscher, R., Angelo, T. A., & White, P. J. (2024). *Mapping design stages and methodologies for developing STEM concept inventories: a scoping review*. Frontiers in Education, 9, 1442833. https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2024.1442833  
  Key use: five-stage CI development framework; student interviews, expert input, CTT/IRT practices.

- Cetnar, A. J. (2023). *Introduction to concept inventories for medical physics education*. Journal of Applied Clinical Medical Physics. https://doi.org/10.1002/acm2.14130  
  Key use: CTT/IRT validation framing and multi-institutional accrual need.

- Artino, A. R. Jr., et al. (2014). *Developing questionnaires for educational research: AMEE Guide No. 87*. Medical Teacher. (Open version in PMC.)  
  Key use: cognitive interviewing as response-process validity evidence.

- Polit, D. F., & Beck, C. T. (2006). *The content validity index: are you sure you know what's being reported? Critique and recommendations*. Research in Nursing & Health. https://doi.org/10.1002/nur.20147  
  Key use: I-CVI/S-CVI interpretation and reporting clarity.

## Item analysis

- Physics Inventory of Quantitative Literacy validation literature (Phys. Rev. Phys. Educ. Res., 2021): CTT item difficulty/discrimination heuristics and reliability framing. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.17.020129

- Tarrant et al. / later reviews of non-functioning distractors: options selected by very small fractions (often <5%) require inspection rather than automatic retention. Use distractor functioning as empirical evidence, not a rigid law.

## Pretest sensitization / parallel forms

- Willson, V. L., & Putnam, R. R. (1982). *A Meta-analysis of Pretest Sensitization Effects in Experimental Design*. American Educational Research Journal, 19(2), 249–258. https://doi.org/10.3102/00028312019002249

- All, A., et al. (2017). *Pre-test influences on the effectiveness of digital-game based learning: A case study of a fire safety game*. Computers & Education, 114, 24–37. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2017.05.018

- Solomon four-group methodological literature can be used if the study is specifically designed to estimate pretest sensitization; for the first INFN implementation, calibrated parallel forms and counterbalancing are more practical.

## Engagement

- O’Brien, H. L., Cairns, P., & Hall, M. (2018). *A practical approach to measuring user engagement with the refined User Engagement Scale (UES) and new UES short form*. International Journal of Human-Computer Studies, 112, 28–39. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2018.01.004

## Scientific reference for item keys

- IAEA Human Health Series material on PET: positron emitters, positron-electron annihilation and 511 keV photon pairs.
- IAEA training material on therapeutic radiopharmaceuticals: beta-minus, alpha and Auger emitters.
- IAEA *Radiation, People and the Environment*: natural radionuclides including K-40 and C-14, radon-related natural exposure.

Use primary/authoritative nuclear-data and medical-physics references during final expert review of every key; the educational literature supports the misconceptions, not all nuclear-science details.

## Authoritative references added for irradiation / activation items

- U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA). *Food Irradiation: What You Need to Know*. The FDA states that food irradiation under approved conditions does not make food radioactive. https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/food-irradiation-what-you-need-know  
  Key use: Explorer practical item E10.

- International Atomic Energy Agency. *Natural and Induced Radioactivity in Food* (IAEA-TECDOC-1287). The report distinguishes ordinary approved food-irradiation conditions from higher-energy conditions capable of inducing measurable activation. https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/te_1287_prn.pdf  
  Key use: Scientist activation nuance S13.

- International Atomic Energy Agency. PET/CT radiation-protection guidance. PET patients containing positron-emitting radiopharmaceuticals are external radiation sources because of 511 keV annihilation photons; exposure of another person is conceptually distinct from contamination or incorporation of radionuclide.  
  Key use: Explorer E10 practical exposure scenario and Scientist PET items.
